# Food Industry Knowledge

## Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) / Safety Data Sheet (SDS)

A Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS)—now commonly called a Safety Data Sheet (SDS)—is a technical document providing comprehensive safety, health, and environmental information about a chemical product.

**Key Information Included in MSDS/SDS:**
- **Identification**: Product name, manufacturer, and emergency contact details
- **Hazards Identification**: Physical risks (flammability, reactivity) and health risks (toxicity, carcinogen effects)
- **Composition**: Information on ingredients and chemical properties
- **First-Aid Measures**: Action to take in case of exposure or injury
- **Handling and Storage**: Safe handling practices, storage requirements, and stability data
- **Exposure Controls/Personal Protection**: Recommended protective equipment (PPE) like masks or gloves
- **Accidental Release Measures**: Procedures for cleaning spills or leaks
- **Disposal Considerations**: Proper environmental disposal methods

**Purpose**: Under GHS standards, MSDS/SDS documents are designed to minimize risks by providing essential information for chemical workers, hazardous materials handlers, and emergency responders.

**In Food Industry Context**:
- Required for **hazardous chemical substances** (cleaning chemicals, sanitizers, processing aids)
- **NOT required** for food products themselves (fruits, vegetables, finished food products)
- Companies may declare that their food products "do not require MSDS" as they are non-hazardous

## REACH Regulation

A REACH statement is a document or declaration confirming that a product, substance, or article complies with the European Union's "Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation, and Restriction of Chemicals" (REACH) regulation (EC 1907/2006).

**Purpose**: Ensures that hazardous substances are identified, managed, and restricted to protect human health and the environment.

**Key Elements**:
- **Registration**: Companies must register chemical substances they manufacture or import
- **Evaluation**: Authorities evaluate the safety of chemicals
- **Authorisation**: Restricted chemicals need special authorization for use
- **Restriction**: Certain chemicals may be banned or limited
- **SVHC**: Substances of Very High Concern - chemicals that pose serious risks

**In Food Industry Context**:
- Food companies may issue REACH compliance statements
- Typically declaring their food products **do not contain SVHC** (Substances of Very High Concern)
- Common for fruit/vegetable products to declare "no MSDS required under REACH" (non-hazardous)
- This is a **regulatory compliance declaration**, not a operational procedure

## Questionnaire Structure

- **Supplier**: The company being evaluated (fills out questionnaires)
- **Customer**: The company requesting the questionnaire (evaluates suppliers)

A supplier receives questionnaires from multiple customers.

**Entity Level**: Questionnaires are about a specific entity (factory/site), not individual products:
- Certifications (Halal, Kosher, IFS, BRC, Organic, etc.) → Entity level
- Food safety systems (HACCP) → Entity level
- Contact details, addresses → Entity level

If a factory says it's Halal certified, ALL products from that facility are Halal. Certifications don't vary per product within the same entity.

**When answers conflict** across questionnaires for the same entity, it's likely:
1. An error in one of the questionnaires
2. Change over time (certification gained or lost)
3. Misunderstanding of the question

It is NOT because different products have different certifications.

## GFSI Standards

Global Food Safety Initiative (GFSI) recognized standards. Having one is often required to supply major retailers.

| Standard | Full Name | Common In |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| BRC | BRCGS (BRC Global Standard for Food Safety) | UK retail |
| IFS | IFS Food (International Featured Standards) | Germany, France |
| FSSC 22000 | Food Safety System Certification 22000 | Manufacturing |
| SQF | Safe Quality Food | North America, Australia |

**Note**: BRCGS = BRC Global Standards = BRC. Same thing, different names.

### BRC Standard Variants

BRC/BRCGS has multiple standards for different types of operations:

| Standard | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **BRC Food** | Food manufacturing (the main GFSI standard) |
| **BRC Agents & Brokers** | Trading companies that don't physically handle products |
| **BRC Storage & Distribution** | Warehousing and logistics |
| **BRC Packaging** | Packaging materials manufacturing |

**Important**: A trading company (agent/broker) would have "BRC Agents & Brokers" not "BRC Food". These are different certifications for different activities.

|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| SEDEX | Supplier Ethical Data Exchange | Platform for sharing ethical data. Members get a number (e.g., ZC408415203) |
| SMETA | Sedex Members Ethical Trade Audit | The audit methodology used by SEDEX members |

| SA 8000 | Social Accountability 8000 | Based on ILO conventions |
| ICS | Initiative for Compliance and Sustainability | French social compliance |

### SEDEX vs SMETA - Important Distinction

- **SEDEX** = Platform/membership. A company "is a SEDEX member" and has a membership number.
- **SMETA** = Audit methodology. A company "has been SMETA audited" or "is SMETA certified".

These are TWO SEPARATE questions:
1. "Are you a SEDEX member?" → Yes/No + membership number
2. "Have you been SMETA audited?" → Yes/No + audit details

A company can be a SEDEX member without having a SMETA audit. Do NOT combine these into one question.

## Sustainability

### Supply Chain Models (used by RSPO, RTRS, ProTerra, ISCC, Donau Soya)

Most sustainability certifications use these supply chain models (highest to lowest traceability):

| Model | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| IP | Identity Preserved | Traceable to single source, physically separate |
| SG | Segregated | Certified kept physically separate throughout chain |
| MB | Mass Balance | Mixed with non-certified but tracked administratively |
| BC | Book & Claim | Credits system, no physical link to certified product |

### RSPO (Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil)

Certification for sustainable palm oil. Uses IP, SG, MB, BC models above.

### Soy Certifications

**Why soy and GMO are connected:**
- ~80% of global soy production is genetically modified (USA, Brazil, Argentina)
- European market strongly prefers non-GMO products
- Soy certifications guarantee both sustainability AND GMO status

**For dairy/cheese companies:** Soy questions are about what the COWS are fed. If cows eat GMO soy feed, the milk/cheese cannot be labeled "non-GMO" or "VLOG" certified.

| Certification | Full Name | GMO Policy |
|---------------|-----------|------------|
| **Donau Soja** | Donau Soja | Non-GMO required (Danube region origin) |
| **Europe Soya** | Europe Soya | Non-GMO required (European origin) |
| **ProTerra** | ProTerra Standard | Non-GMO required (<0.1% limit) |
| **RTRS** | Round Table on Responsible Soy | Allows GMO (global standard) |
| **ISCC** | International Sustainability & Carbon Certification | Optional non-GMO add-on |

**Key differences:**
- **Donau Soja / Europe Soya**: European origin, non-GMO, recognized by VLOG
- **ProTerra**: Non-GMO focus, strict 0.1% limit, based on Basel Criteria (WWF/Coop Switzerland)
- **RTRS**: Multi-stakeholder (160+ members), allows GMO, focus on deforestation-free
- **ISCC**: Broader scope (soy, palm, biofuels), carbon certification, EUDR compliance

All use similar supply chain models (IP, SG, MB, BC) for traceability.

### German Feed & Dairy Standards

| Standard | Full Name | Scope |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| **QS** | Qualität und Sicherheit | German quality scheme for food/feed safety |
| **QS-Soja+** | QS Soyplus | QS add-on requiring sustainable soy in feed (since 2024) |
| **QM Milch** | Qualitätsmanagement Milch | German milk quality standard at farm level |
| **GMP+** | Good Manufacturing Practice | International feed safety certification (20,000+ companies) |

**Connection:** QS-Soja+ requires feed to use certified sustainable soy. QM Milch certifies milk production. GMP+ certified feed suppliers can deliver to QM Milch dairy farms.

### FEFAC Soy Sourcing Guidelines

FEFAC = European Compound Feed Manufacturers' Federation. Not a certification itself, but a **benchmarking tool** that other soy certifications are measured against. If a certification is "FEFAC compliant", it meets European feed industry requirements for responsible soy.

## EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation)

**Full name**: EU Deforestation Regulation (Regulation 2023/1115)

**Purpose**: Prevents products linked to deforestation from entering the EU market.

**Cut-off date**: December 31, 2020 - land must not have been deforested after this date.

**Seven commodities covered**:
1. Cattle (and beef, leather)
2. Cocoa (and chocolate)
3. Coffee
4. Oil palm (and palm oil)
5. Rubber
6. Soya (and soy products)
7. Wood (and wood products, paper, printed materials)

**Timeline**:
- Originally December 2024, now **December 30, 2025** for large companies
- December 30, 2026 for SMEs

**Due diligence requirements**:
- Traceability to plot of land where commodities were produced
- Geolocation data required
- Risk assessment and mitigation

**Relevance for dairy/cheese**: If products contain soy (e.g., in feed) or palm oil, EUDR compliance may apply.

### Codex Alimentarius

International food standards established by FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization) and WHO (World Health Organization).

**Key facts**:
- 188 member countries + EU
- Develops international food standards, guidelines, and codes of practice
- Reference for WTO food trade disputes
- Covers food labeling, additives, contaminants, hygiene, residues

**Common references**:
- Codex HACCP principles
- Codex General Principles of Food Hygiene
- Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) for pesticides/veterinary drugs

When questionnaires ask "Do you follow Codex Alimentarius principles?" they typically mean HACCP and general hygiene standards.

## EU Allergens (14 Mandatory)

Under EU 1169/2011, these 14 allergens must be declared if present:

| # | Allergen | Examples |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Cereals containing gluten** | Wheat, rye, barley, oats, spelt, khorasan |
| 2 | **Crustaceans** | Shrimp, crab, lobster |
| 3 | **Eggs** | All egg products |
| 4 | **Fish** | All fish species |
| 5 | **Peanuts** | Groundnuts |
| 6 | **Soybeans** | Soy products |
| 7 | **Milk** | Including lactose |
| 8 | **Tree nuts** | Almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamia |
| 9 | **Celery** | Including celeriac |
| 10 | **Mustard** | Seeds, powder, oil |
| 11 | **Sesame seeds** | Including oil |
| 12 | **Sulphites** | >10 mg/kg SO₂ |
| 13 | **Lupin** | Lupine seeds, flour |
| 14 | **Molluscs** | Mussels, oysters, squid, snails |

**Allergen questionnaire terminology**:
- "Present" / "Intentionally added" = Contains the allergen as ingredient
- "Cross-contamination" / "May contain" = Risk of traces from shared equipment
- "Absent in facility" = Allergen never present in the production site

**Gluten-free**: <20 ppm gluten (per EU 828/2014)

## General Regulatory Statements

Food companies often issue general statements to declare regulatory compliance. Based on EU regulations, these statements should contain specific elements and be supported by corresponding procedures.

### General Allergen Statement

**Regulation**: EU 1169/2011 (Food Information to Consumers)

**Purpose**: Declare allergen status of products - either allergen-free or presence declaration.

**Required Elements**:
- Reference to EU Regulation 1169/2011
- Declaration for all 14 mandatory allergens (see EU Allergens section above)
- Specific naming of tree nuts if applicable
- Clear identification of products containing allergens
- Documentation of dedicated facilities where applicable
- Exceptions clearly stated (e.g., specific concentrates)

**Supporting Procedures Expected**:
- Allergen management procedure
- Allergen risk assessment
- Cross-contamination prevention procedures
- Allergen labeling procedures
- Supplier allergen declarations
- Staff training on allergen handling
- Cleaning validation for allergen removal

**Key Terms for Matching**: allergen, allergenic, allergy, cross-contamination

### General Product Statement

**Regulations**: Multiple EU Food Safety Regulations (EC 178/2002, EU 1935/2004, EU 2073/2005, EU 2023/915, etc.)

**Purpose**: Declare comprehensive product compliance covering food safety, quality standards, and regulatory requirements.

**Required Elements**:
- Compliance with EU food safety regulations
- Product safety declarations
- Quality policy statements
- Contamination prevention measures
- Packaging material compliance (EU 1935/2004)
- Microbiological criteria compliance (EU 2073/2005)
- Maximum contaminant levels compliance (EU 2023/915)
- Specific substance declarations (melamine-free, hormone-free, etc.)
- Restrictions on additives, colorants, fumigation

**Supporting Procedures Expected**:
- Product quality control procedures
- Contamination monitoring procedures
- Packaging material approval procedures
- Microbiological testing procedures
- Chemical analysis procedures
- Product specification procedures
- Ingredient approval procedures
- Production hygiene procedures

**Key Terms for Matching**: product standard, product compliance, product specification, product quality, product safety

### General MSDS Statement

**Regulation**: EU REACH Regulation (EC) 1907/2006

**Purpose**: Declare that food products do not require Material Safety Data Sheets as they are not hazardous chemical substances.

**Required Elements**:
- Declaration that products do not require MSDS
- Confirmation products are not classified as dangerous substances
- Reference to REACH regulation compliance
- Statement that products are food ingredients, not chemicals
- Clarification of regulatory classification

**Supporting Procedures Expected**:
- Product classification procedures
- Chemical substance evaluation procedures
- Regulatory compliance assessment procedures
- Safety documentation procedures

**Key Terms for Matching**: msds, material safety data sheet, safety data sheet, reach, dangerous substance

### General Pesticide Residues Statement

**Regulation**: EU Regulation (EC) 396/2005 (Maximum Residue Levels)

**Purpose**: Declare compliance with maximum residue levels of pesticides in food and feed of plant and animal origin.

**Required Elements**:
- Declaration of pesticide residue compliance
- Reference to EU Regulation 396/2005 and maximum residue levels (MRLs)
- Monitoring and testing protocols
- Supplier pesticide declarations
- Traceability of raw materials
- HACCP integration for pesticide control
- Regular residue testing results

**Supporting Procedures Expected**:
- Pesticide residue monitoring procedures
- Supplier pesticide assessment procedures
- Raw material testing procedures
- Pesticide residue analysis procedures
- MRL compliance procedures
- Non-conformance handling procedures
- Traceability procedures for contamination sources

**Key Terms for Matching**: pesticide, residue, maximum residue level, mrl, contamination monitoring, chemical analysis

### Using General Statements for Procedure Matching

When matching general regulatory statements to procedure questions in answer libraries:

1. **Identify statement type** by keywords in document title
2. **Use key terms** to find relevant procedure questions
3. **Expect supporting procedures** as listed above for each statement type
4. **Check regulatory compliance** - missing procedures may indicate compliance gaps
5. **Apply to any food company** - these requirements are industry-wide, not company-specific
